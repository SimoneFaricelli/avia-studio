How to Assess Employees' AI Skills: 5 Practical Tests for the Workplace

Learn how to assess employees' AI skills with five practical workplace tests, a scoring rubric and examples for evaluating real AI capabilities.

27 October 2026 10 min read

An employee completes an AI training course.

They pass the final quiz.

They receive a certificate.

But can they actually use AI to solve a problem at work?

That is a different question.

Knowing what generative AI is does not necessarily mean knowing when to use it, how to evaluate its output or how to apply it to a real professional task.

This creates a challenge for companies investing in employee AI training.

How can you assess employees’ AI skills in a way that measures practical capability rather than memorized knowledge?

The answer is to combine basic knowledge checks with realistic workplace exercises.

In this guide, we’ll walk through five practical AI skills assessment tests, explain how to score them and show how to turn the results into more relevant employee training.

Why Traditional AI Knowledge Quizzes Are Not Enough

Multiple-choice quizzes can be useful.

They can help determine whether employees understand:

  • What generative AI is.
  • Why AI-generated information can be inaccurate.
  • Basic prompting principles.
  • Responsible AI practices.
  • Common AI capabilities and limitations.

But quizzes have an important limitation.

An employee may know the correct answer to a question without being able to apply that knowledge.

Consider two questions.

Knowledge question:

“Why is it important to verify AI-generated information?”

Practical question:

“Review this AI-generated summary, compare it with the source document and identify any unsupported claims.”

The first measures conceptual understanding.

The second measures whether an employee can demonstrate the skill.

Both are useful, but they provide different evidence.

A stronger assessment strategy uses both.

Test 1: Can the Employee Write an Effective AI Prompt?

Skill being assessed: Prompting and task communication.

Give the employee a realistic workplace task.

For example:

“You need to prepare a short internal email announcing a change to the weekly team meeting schedule.”

Ask the employee to write a prompt that would help an AI assistant produce an appropriate first draft.

A strong prompt might include:

  • The intended audience.
  • The purpose of the message.
  • The relevant schedule details.
  • The desired tone.
  • The expected length.
  • Any important constraints.

Example

Weak prompt:

“Write an email about a meeting.”

Stronger prompt:

“Draft a concise, professional email to our internal team announcing that the weekly Monday meeting has moved from 9:00 AM to 10:00 AM. Explain that the change begins next week. Keep the message under 120 words and use a friendly, direct tone.”

The second prompt gives the AI more useful information.

How to Score the Test

Evaluate whether the employee:

  1. Clearly defines the task.
  2. Provides relevant context.
  3. Specifies the intended output.
  4. Includes important constraints.
  5. Avoids unnecessary ambiguity.

Do not score the employee simply by how long their prompt is.

The objective is clear communication, not prompt complexity.

Test 2: Can the Employee Identify AI Mistakes?

Skill being assessed: Output evaluation and critical thinking.

Provide the employee with a short source document and an AI-generated summary.

Include two or three deliberate errors in the summary.

The AI-generated summary might incorrectly state:

Ask the employee to:

  • Identify the incorrect information.
  • Explain why it is incorrect.
  • Correct the summary.
  • Identify any statements that cannot be verified from the source.

What This Test Reveals

An employee who can generate AI content but cannot evaluate it may struggle to use AI reliably.

This matters because fluent, confident-sounding AI responses are not necessarily accurate.

Employees need to understand that important outputs require appropriate review.

How to Score the Test

Assess:

  1. Detection of factual errors.
  2. Comparison with source material.
  3. Recognition of unsupported claims.
  4. Accuracy of corrections.
  5. Explanation of remaining uncertainty.

This test can be adapted to different departments using fictional, non-sensitive documents.

Test 3: Can the Employee Recognize When AI Should Not Be Used?

Skill being assessed: Judgment and responsible AI use.

AI capability is not simply the ability to use AI frequently.

It also includes knowing when AI is inappropriate, unnecessary or requires additional safeguards.

Present three workplace scenarios.

Scenario A

An employee wants to summarize a publicly available industry report.

Scenario B

An employee wants to paste confidential customer records into an unapproved public AI tool.

Scenario C

An employee wants AI to draft an initial outline for a routine internal presentation.

Ask the employee to explain whether AI should be used in each situation and why.

What a Strong Answer Looks Like

For Scenario A, AI may be useful, provided the employee verifies the summary.

For Scenario B, the employee should recognize the confidentiality risk and follow organizational policies rather than uploading sensitive information to an unapproved service.

For Scenario C, AI may be useful for creating a first draft, with human review before the presentation is used.

The correct response depends on the company’s policies, approved tools and the nature of the task.

How to Score the Test

Evaluate whether the employee:

  1. Recognizes potential risks.
  2. Understands relevant organizational rules.
  3. Distinguishes appropriate from inappropriate use.
  4. Identifies when human review is necessary.
  5. Explains their decision clearly.

Good AI judgment is not about saying yes to every AI use case.

Test 4: Can the Employee Apply AI to a Realistic Job Task?

Skill being assessed: Practical workplace application.

This is where assessments become more relevant to different roles.

Give employees a task connected to their actual responsibilities.

Sales Example

Provide a fictional company profile and ask the employee to use AI to prepare five relevant discovery questions for an introductory sales meeting.

Evaluate whether the questions are specific, useful and grounded in the provided information.

HR Example

Provide a fictional internal policy document and ask the employee to use an approved AI tool to prepare a clear employee-facing summary.

Evaluate accuracy, clarity and responsible information handling.

Marketing Example

Provide a short collection of fictional customer feedback and ask the employee to identify recurring themes with AI assistance.

Evaluate whether the conclusions are supported by the feedback.

Finance Example

Provide a synthetic financial report and ask the employee to use AI to summarize key changes and identify figures that require further investigation.

Evaluate numerical accuracy and verification.

Why Role-Specific Exercises Matter

A generic AI quiz may tell you whether an employee understands prompting.

A role-specific exercise can help reveal whether they know how to use that skill in relevant work.

This is an important distinction.

Employees do not all need the same AI applications, even when they use the same technology.

Test 5: Can the Employee Improve an AI-Assisted Workflow?

Skill being assessed: Workflow integration and problem-solving.

Give the employee a recurring workplace process.

For example:

  1. Read several documents.
  2. Extract relevant information.
  3. Prepare a short summary.
  4. Review the summary.
  5. Share the final result.

Ask the employee to identify where AI could help and where human involvement remains necessary.

The employee should explain:

  • Which step could benefit from AI.
  • What input the AI would need.
  • What output it should produce.
  • How the result would be verified.
  • Which steps should remain under human control.

Example Answer

An employee might propose using an approved AI tool to create an initial summary of non-sensitive documents.

They would then compare the generated summary against the originals, correct inaccuracies and approve the final version before sharing it.

This is a stronger demonstration of capability than simply opening an AI assistant and generating text.

How to Score the Test

Assess:

  1. Identification of a relevant use case.
  2. Appropriateness of the proposed AI application.
  3. Understanding of inputs and outputs.
  4. Quality-control measures.
  5. Clarity of human responsibilities.

The objective is not to automate everything.

It is to improve a process where AI can contribute meaningfully.

A Simple AI Skills Assessment Scoring Rubric

Companies can use a common scoring framework across all five tests.

Here is an illustrative four-level rubric.

Score Skill Level Description
0 Not demonstrated The employee cannot demonstrate the required capability.
1 Basic The employee demonstrates partial understanding but needs substantial guidance.
2 Competent The employee can complete routine tasks with appropriate review.
3 Proficient The employee demonstrates consistent capability, sound judgment and effective application.

Each test receives a score from 0 to 3.

With five equally weighted tests, the maximum total is 15 points.

However, companies should not rely exclusively on the total score.

How to Turn Assessment Results Into Personalized AI Training

Once employees have completed the assessment, the next step is to identify what they should learn.

A practical approach involves four stages.

1. Identify the Gaps

Compare demonstrated skills with the capabilities required for the employee’s role.

Do not assume every employee needs to reach the highest level in every category.

2. Prioritize Relevant Capabilities

Focus first on skills that are important for the employee’s actual responsibilities.

For example, someone frequently reviewing AI-generated reports may need strong output evaluation skills.

3. Assign Targeted Learning Activities

Match each important gap with a relevant training activity.

For example:

Gap: Difficulty identifying unsupported AI claims.

Learning activity: A short lesson on AI output verification.

Practice: Compare AI-generated summaries with original source documents.

Assessment: Identify and correct deliberately introduced errors.

4. Reassess After Training

Repeat a comparable exercise after the learning activity.

This can help determine whether the employee’s demonstrated capability has improved.

Use a different but equivalent test scenario to reduce the chance of measuring memorization.

How Often Should Companies Assess AI Skills?

There is no universal schedule.

A practical starting point could include:

  • An initial assessment before training.
  • A follow-up assessment after relevant learning activities.
  • Periodic reassessment when responsibilities, tools or required capabilities change.

Companies should also consider whether employees have opportunities to apply their new skills.

An employee may demonstrate a capability during an assessment but rarely need it in their actual role.

This is why skills assessment and workplace adoption should be evaluated separately.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Measuring Only Theoretical Knowledge

Knowledge quizzes can provide useful information, but they should not be the only evidence of practical capability.

Giving Everyone the Same Assessment

Some foundational skills can be assessed consistently across the organization.

However, practical exercises should reflect different responsibilities.

Using a Single Score to Define an Employee

An overall score can hide important weaknesses.

Skill profiles provide more actionable information.

Ignoring Responsible AI Use

An employee who produces impressive outputs but cannot recognize inappropriate data handling may require further training.

Assessing Employees Without Changing Their Training

Assessment results are most useful when they influence what employees learn next.

Confusing Assessment Performance With Adoption

Passing a practical test demonstrates capability under assessment conditions.

It does not prove that an employee regularly uses that capability at work.

From Testing Knowledge to Developing Real AI Capabilities

The purpose of an AI skills assessment should not be to create another administrative requirement.

It should help answer three questions:

What can this employee already do?

What do they need to improve?

What should they learn next?

When companies assess practical capabilities, they can move beyond generic AI courses and build training that responds to actual employee needs.

That is a more useful starting point for workforce AI development.

Instead of measuring only how much employees know about AI, organizations can begin evaluating what employees are capable of doing with it.
